A photo goes through the same door as a video. On the media screen tap
**photo** instead of the video button, pick a shot from your ride, and the
app matches it to the ride the same way: by the time the photo was taken.
Then the photo editor opens instead of the video one.

That means the same rule as for video: use the original. A photo that came
to you on WhatsApp, Instagram or Messenger is a recompressed copy dated when
it arrived, so it won't find the ride on its own; ask for it as a document
or by AirDrop instead. See
[sent through a messaging app](/help/troubleshooting/data-doesnt-line-up/#sent-through-a-messaging-app).

Nearly everything is the same. Same stickers, same drag, pinch and
double-tap, same style panel and eyedropper, same alignment strip if the
numbers are from the wrong moment. Two things are different.

## Photo stickers show one moment

A video sticker ticks along with the footage. A photo sticker shows a single
sample, the second the shutter went. That makes alignment matter more, not
less: on a video a two-second offset is invisible, on a photo it's the
difference between the speed at the top of the climb and the speed on the
descent. If the number doesn't match what you remember, swipe up and drag
the window on the elevation strip until it does. The
[alignment page](/help/troubleshooting/data-doesnt-line-up/) covers it.

Because of that, the **totals** stickers are the ones that really suit a
photo: total distance, total climbing, ride time and average speed. They
read the whole ride rather than a moment, so there's nothing to align, and
they say what a photo caption usually says anyway. They work on video too,
where the number just stays constant.

## Story or original

A toggle in the editor switches the canvas.

**Story** is 9:16 portrait, whatever shape the photo is. The photo sits
inside the frame and you can pinch to zoom and drag to pan it; it won't zoom
out smaller than the frame and it won't pan so far that an edge comes
inside. Double-tap the photo to fill the frame, double-tap again to fit it.
On export the empty bars above and below are filled with a blurred, scaled
copy of the same photo, which is the look Instagram gives a landscape shot in
a Story. The output is 1080 by 1920.

**Original** keeps the photo's own shape and size. Stickers stay inside the
photo's bounds. What you export is the photo's own pixels with the overlay
on top.

Switching from story to original pulls any sticker you'd parked in a bar
back onto the photo, so nothing ends up off-frame.

## Export and share

Tap create and the overlay is composited in one go; there's no video render,
so it's quick. Then it's the usual share page: system share, Instagram
(Feed for a photo), TikTok, or save to the Cycladat album. A photo from a
Strava ride carries the "Powered by Strava" mark like a video does. Leaving
the share page deletes the export, so save or share before you go.
